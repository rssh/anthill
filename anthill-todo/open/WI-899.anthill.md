## description

A RULE CLAUSE ON A BUILTIN-BACKED NAME IS INERT AT SLD, AND NOTHING SAYS SO. Once WI-896 made a rule head functor resolve rather than declare, a head naming an already-resolving name contributes a clause to that symbol — and if the symbol carries a resolver BuiltinTag, the builtin decides the goal before any clause is consulted. The clause loads clean and is never reached.
MEASURED (`wi896_labeled_predicate_head_test::a_clause_on_a_builtin_backed_name_is_inert_at_sld`, which pins today's behaviour and will FAIL when this lands): with `rule or(?a) :- p896(?a)` and `rule or(?a, ?b) :- p896(?a), p896(?b)` in one namespace, the 1-ary goal yields the user's clause (1 solution) while the 2-ary goal yields the kernel disjunction's choice points and never reaches the 2-ary clause. So ARITY decides, silently: the clause is dead exactly at the builtin's own arity.
NOT INTRODUCED BY WI-896, WHICH IS WHY IT IS ITS OWN TICKET. The smt-gen `rule bound: gte(?x, 3.0) :- gte(?x, 5.0)` lemma has ALWAYS been a clause on the builtin-backed `anthill.prelude.PartialOrd.gte` — inert at SLD, cited only by the SMT backend, and that is the intended use. So the refusal cannot be 'head resolves to a builtin': that criterion refuses the 9 smt-gen/prove targets. WI-896 only widened WHICH names reach the state (a bare `or`/`not` head now joins the labeled ones), while REMOVING a worse silence in the same breath — the old behaviour minted `<ns>.or` and re-pointed every bare `or` in the scope, measured at 0 solutions for a disjunction two lines down.
WHAT THIS TICKET MUST DECIDE is which of the two uses is which, since the source looks identical: a citable LEMMA about a builtin-backed operation (keep, silent inertness is correct) versus an attempted DEFINITION the resolver will never run (refuse, or diagnose). Candidate discriminators, none settled: the target's SymbolKind (`Operation` for `gte`, and `anthill.kernel.or`/`push_choice`/`cut`/`find_dictionary` are control primitives that are not operations at all); whether the clause's arity equals the builtin's; whether the rule is cited by any `using`. Note `eq`/`unify`/`struct_eq` must stay permitted in head position regardless — `field.anthill` writes three bodied equation-shaped rules whose head functor IS the connective.
ACCEPTANCE: the two uses are told apart by a stated rule; a clause the resolver can never reach is refused or diagnosed rather than silently loaded; the smt-gen policy renders and the `prove_*` family still pass; full workspace green. REFERENCE: WI-896 (which made the head resolve), WI-523/WI-526 (the original 'a user's own `or` must not merge with the kernel primitive' concern, now measured), WI-895 (the sibling silent-inertness gap in a rule BODY).
