## description

Replace 059 R3's BLANKET rule ban in a secondary entry with the NARROW rule the
proposal actually intends. Spec: docs/proposals/059-secondary-entries.md, "The rule a
secondary entry MAY declare, and the two conditions that make it sound".

A rule is a definition, and a definition of something NEW displaces nothing. So a
secondary entry may declare a rule exactly when BOTH hold:
  (1) ITS HEAD INTRODUCES — the head resolves to nothing in the FINISHED program, so no
      existing goal can be about it; and
  (2) ONE ENTRY OWNS THE PREDICATE — every clause of that head is written in that same
      entry, keyed as 059's Definitions individuate one: the main entry, or one file's
      text at that address.

EACH CONDITION ANSWERS A MEASURED FAILURE of the naive "a fresh head is harmless" reading:
  * WITHOUT (2), two entries silently compose one predicate. Two secondary entries IN
    DIFFERENT FILES that each introduce `freshp` do not collide — both clauses join it,
    `freshp(1)` and `freshp(2)` both answering. Neither captured a pre-existing name, so
    R4 is silent, and the predicate ends up assembled by two parties that never agreed on
    it. R4 CANNOT express this, because a predicate legitimately has many clauses; it is
    a rule about where they may be WRITTEN. Two blocks in ONE file are one entry and
    compose freely — deliberately: that is one author writing two paragraphs.
  * WITHOUT (1), the head is not really new. A main entry carrying
    `rule q(0) :- not freshp(1)` answers q(0) once; add `namespace Rec { rule freshp(1) }`
    and it answers ZERO times, though the head was as fresh as heads get — the symbol
    lands at X.freshp, exactly where the main entry would have put it. The REFERENCE
    existed before the DEFINITION did.

WHY IT CANNOT LAND YET — the two gates, both already ticketed:
  * WI-980 (R6). Condition (1) is UNDECIDABLE while text order decides whether a head
    introduces. Measured today, `rule p(1)` then `sort Rec { rule p(2) }` joins both
    clauses to one `p`, while the same two swapped make TWO predicates. R6 says the
    binding is computed against the FINISHED program; until it is, (1) has no stable
    answer to compute.
  * WI-895. Condition (1) is UNSOUND while a body may reference what resolves to nothing.
    Measured, a rule body and a constraint both LOAD while naming a predicate that
    resolves to nothing (an operation body naming one is already refused, `unknown
    functor`). A name that resolves to nothing must stop being referenceable before
    "resolves to nothing" can mean "no one refers to it".

NO FURTHER DESIGN IS NEEDED. Both conditions are decidable at scan time once the gates
close — (2) by grouping a predicate's clauses on (address, SourceId) with the main entry
as its own group, which is the key the declaration ledger already carries.

TWO SHAPES ARE EXCLUDED BY (1) RATHER THAN BY A CLAUSE OF THEIR OWN, which is the sign
the condition is the right one: a DOT RULE and an OPERATOR RULE carry the desugar's
functor (dot_apply, add), and a desugared conclusion introduces nothing — so neither is
ever fresh, and the `[simp]`-fires-in-the-typer hazard cannot arise through them.

ONE HONEST LIMIT, do not let this ticket be read as closing it: this does NOT close the
underlying hazard. A rule extending a predicate declared elsewhere is NON-MONOTONE
wherever it is written — an ordinary nested namespace does the same, and so does the
sort's own body. R3 closes the route 059 opens; the general case belongs with the module
question.
