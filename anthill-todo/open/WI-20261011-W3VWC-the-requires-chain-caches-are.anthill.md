## Description

THE REQUIRES-CHAIN CACHES ARE STILL INVALIDATED BY HAND AROUND EACH PASS THAT ASSERTS A PROVISION — the stale-cache class WI-954 names, one level up from the provider index WI-20261010-ANK4X took out of it. Decide whether a provision asserted invalidates them where it is stored, and build that with its control. Found by /code-review of ANK4X (2026-10-11), not fixed there: outside that ticket.

WHAT STANDS TODAY. `direct_requires` reads provisions since WI-1110 (`self_supplied_entries`), so the requires chain and everything laid out from it — `requires_chain_cache`, `requires_tree_cache`, `synth_req_names_cache`, `provider_dict_chain_cache`, `provision_layout_key_cache`, `provision_member_cache`, and the op-keyed half (`op_requires_chain_cache`, `synth_op_req_names_cache`, `op_dict_chain_cache`, `op_frame_names_cache`) — is a memo over the provision relation as well as over the requires relation. `KnowledgeBase::invalidate_requires_chain_cache` clears them, and drops `requires_index`. It has FOUR call sites outside tests: before and after `derive_forwarded_provisions` and after `eq_derive::run` in `load::load_phase_inner`, and in `sort_domain_derive::run`. The other passes that assert provisions — `derive_total_eq`, `derive_conditional_eq`, `type_value_derive::run` — call it nowhere and are covered by standing ABOVE the first of those four in the pipeline. load.rs says of two of the calls that no entry can actually change and that they are made anyway, "because 'this particular producer happens not to matter' is the reasoning that leaves a stale cache behind the next one (WI-954)". A new derivation pass written to rustland/CLAUDE.md as ANK4X left it owes the provider index nothing and still owes this call; the file says so, and nothing holds a pass to it.

WHAT A STALE ENTRY IS. Not a slow answer: a chain computed before a provision existed lacks the slot the provision supplies (or keeps one a conversion has since relocated), so a dictionary is laid out wrong — a program that should be refused loads clean and dies at eval on an unbound `__req_*` (WI-954's measured mode), or the reverse.

WHAT TO DECIDE AND BUILD. ANK4X made `KnowledgeBase::push_value_head_entry` the one place a provision fact is seen being stored (`typing::file_provision_fact`) and three places the one where it stops being a row (`unfile_provision_fact`). The caches can be cleared from the same hooks, and the four hand calls and the placement argument go. To settle, each at its site: (1) WHICH memos a PROVISION invalidates and which only a `requires` fact does. `requires_index` indexes `SortRequiresInfo` and is dropped by the same call today only because the two had one invalidation point; a provision asserted should not drop it (it is rebuilt at two fixed points of a load, and a drop between them is the scan WI-1112 removed). So the one function probably becomes two questions. (2) THE COST, measured: a stdlib load asserts about 430 provision rows, and the clear is ten `RefCell<HashMap>` clears a time, most of them of an empty map during the file-loading pass — the bench before and after, full load and later load. If it shows, a single "provisions changed" stamp the caches compare (the shape `SymbolTable::canonical_moves` gave the provider index's keys) is the other way. (3) The `requires` side: whether `SortRequiresInfo` asserts and the retract-and-reassert in `resolve_requires_bindings` should invalidate from the assert path too, or stay as they are — its producers are two, both before the typer; say which and why. (4) What a layer's discard and `deep_clone` do with each memo: a layer scopes them, and `deep_clone` STARTS THEM EMPTY in a copy — its header says so of `requires_chain_cache` and its eight siblings and of `resolve_cache`, their rows holding an `Rc` — so a test on the shared base begins every load with none of them, whatever this ticket does to the invalidation.

FIRST, MEASURE WHAT THE HAND CALLS HOLD — before building anything. load.rs argues at two of the four sites that no entry can change today: the rows `derive_forwarded_provisions` adds are skipped by `self_supplied_entries` (a row `derived_provision_origin_of` recognizes), and the rows `eq_derive::run` adds are concrete-carrier ones and never conversions. If that is so of all four, no program NEEDS the invalidation today, backing each call out fails nothing, and what the hook buys is the next producer and the placement argument retired — worth knowing before the cost of a clear a row is paid. So: back each of the four hand calls out, one at a time, anthill-core's suite under each; say which tests fail for each and which calls nothing in the suite holds. That table decides how much of the rest is built, with the user.

THE CONTROL, for what is built. As for ANK4X, a value is not it — a stale chain shows only where a slot differs. (1) A check, in a build with debug assertions, at the exits of a load: each memoized chain equal to the chain computed afresh. Its cost measured; if it is too dear for every load, a switch for tests instead, with a control of its own that says in the run's log whether it took. (2) A fixture that NEEDS the invalidation, if the measurement above finds or can construct one — a provision asserted after a chain that reads it was memoized, asserting the VALUE of a call through the slot it changes, in one call and as a later load; and if none can be constructed today, say so at the site, with the nearest shape tried. With the hook in and the hand calls gone, back the hook out and say what fails. (3) The gate green under all three recipes.

RELATED, filed the same day: `check_provider_requires` is 42 % of a later load, and the chains it walks are the ones these caches hold — `load_phase_inner` clears them unconditionally at every load, and a copy of the base starts without them. Whether that is where its time goes is that ticket's first measurement; if it is, what is decided here about WHEN the caches are cleared, and whether a copy carries them, is the lever there.

DONE WHEN: the four hand calls' back-outs are measured and written down, and what to build is decided on them; if the hook is built — a provision stored or unfiled invalidates what is memoized over the relation without its producer saying so, the hand calls that were for provisions are gone, what a `requires` fact invalidates is decided and written, the cost is measured into docs/measurements/, and rustland/CLAUDE.md's paragraph on the provider index no longer says a producer owes `invalidate_requires_chain_cache`; the gate is green under all three recipes.
