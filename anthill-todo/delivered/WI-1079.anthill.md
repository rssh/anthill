## Description

A SKOLEM HAS NO REFLECT REPRESENTATION, AND `TypeExtractor.TypeVar` CANNOT CARRY RIGIDITY — the flex/rigid distinction the typer runs on is invisible to the reflect layer.

WI-1063 opens a return's existential slot to a fresh skolem at each use, minted by
`UnwrittenFill::Anonymous` as a bare `Term::Var(Var::Rigid(vid))`. MEASURED by
constructing both carriers and calling `extract_type`:

  Term::Var(Var::Rigid(vid))    ->  TypeExtractor::Error
  Term::Var(Var::Global(vid))   ->  TypeExtractor::Error

`type_head` classifies on the FUNCTOR SYMBOL (`let Some(f) = head.functor_sym() else {
return TypeHead::Error }`), and a bare variable term has none. So a skolem — a
perfectly well-formed type, an opaque constant — is reported as a MALFORMED TERM, and
`TypeExtractor::Error(term)`'s own doc calls its payload "the offending input". The
`extract` totality claim ("keeps `extract` total rather than returning an `Option`") is
satisfied in the letter and broken in the spirit: total, but with the wrong answer.

TWO REPRESENTATIONS OF ONE CONCEPT, and only one of them is reflectable:

  * ENGINE:  `Term::Var(Var::Global | Var::Rigid)` — what unification and the WI-1063
    opening actually manipulate. `Var::Rigid`'s own doc names it: "Equivalent to
    'Skolem constant' (resolution literature) or 'eigenvariable' (sequent calculus)."
  * REFLECT: `TypeExtractor.TypeVar(name: Symbol)` — a deep entity minted by
    `make_type_var`, carrying A NAME AND NOTHING ELSE.

THE FIELD THAT IS MISSING IS RIGIDITY, and that is the finding rather than the mere
`Error`. `TypeVar(name: Symbol)` has no flex/rigid discriminator, so converting a
skolem into one instead of erroring would still LOSE what makes it a skolem: a reflect
consumer cannot tell an OPAQUE CONSTANT (unifies with nothing but itself) from a
UNIFIABLE VARIABLE (binds to anything). That is the same conflation WI-1078 is about —
a named `?E` escaping the existential opening — expressed one level down, in the data
model rather than in the rule.

WHO IS AFFECTED, to be established rather than assumed — the scope question this ticket
must answer FIRST, and the reason it is a ticket rather than an inline fix:

  (a) Does a skolem ever REACH `extract` today? A negative is hard to prove; the
      honest form is to make the `Error` arm LOUD for a variable carrier (a distinct
      diagnostic naming the var and its rigidity) and run all seven corpus tiers plus
      the suite. Silence is then evidence of unreachability, and a hit names its site.
      Note the repo's own rule: prefer a loud error over a silent skip.
  (b) `anthill-cpp-gen` calls `extract_type` directly (`lib.rs:1814`, `:4760`) and
      matches on the variants; what does it do with `Error` today?
  (c) The reflect-layer typing rules in `stdlib/anthill/reflect/typing.anthill` read
      types through this enum. Any of them reasoning about a return's type would see
      `Error` where a skolem stands.

THE BINDER LAYER IS ABSENT ENTIRELY, which decides how the options below relate.
`OperationInfo` (reflect.anthill) carries name, params, return_type, effects, requires,
ensures, meta — and NO `type_params`. An operation's bracket binders (`map[Dst, EffP]`)
are not reflected at all. So "add a quantified type" is not extending a partial
representation; it is introducing the binder layer.

THE TWO DESIGNS ARE NOT PEERS (user's question, 2026-08-11: "introduce Rigid-Vars, or
PolyType with bounded TypeVars?"). A rigid-var form is a COMPONENT of any design with
quantifiers, not a rival to one — opening an existential, or entering a universal's
body, always yields an opaque constant that has to be representable (System F has both
Lambda-bound and free variables). So RIGID-VARS IS NEEDED EITHER WAY and can land first.
What it does NOT give is the quantified form: with rigid-vars alone the reflect layer
can say "this is opaque" but not "this type is forall A. List[A]", so `empty`'s
polymorphism still has no representation and keeps surviving by escape hatches (the
self-sort tie for `List.empty`, the named variable for `LogicalStream.empty` — WI-1078).

A POLYTYPE WITH BOUNDED TYPEVARS IS THE DESIGN THAT RETIRES THE GUESSING, and it
collapses three open tickets into one question:

  WI-1077  carrier parameter INFERRED from operation shapes (four separate
           inferences of one undeclared fact)      -> a BOUNDED binder `A: Iterable`
                                                      says it
  WI-1078  bound-vs-unbound named `?E` decided by SPELLING (named vs anonymous)
                                                   -> structural: is the variable in
                                                      the binder list?
  WI-1079  rigidity unrepresentable                -> the binder carries its
                                                      quantifier; opening discharges
                                                      it to a rigid

That is the user's "specify the quantor in the type" (WI-1078's option 3) given a home
in the data model, and it addresses the root cause the three tickets are symptoms of:
the language keeps INFERRING what it never SAYS. Cost is correspondingly larger — every
`TypeExtractor` reader, `anthill-cpp-gen`'s direct `extract_type` matches, and the new
binder layer on `OperationInfo`.

RECOMMENDED ORDER: rigid-vars first (local, necessary under either design, closes this
ticket's measured defect and gives WI-1078 somewhere to put its answer); PolyType as the
design target, taken as its own ticket with its own measurement, and only if the surface
gains explicit quantifiers — a binder list in reflect that no syntax can write would be
a second undeclared fact rather than a fix.

THE SHAPE, decided with the user 2026-08-11 and grounded in what the language already
spells. Three questions were asked and they collapse into one answer.

  Q: should a PolyType carry ONE quantifier, or a set of (variable, quantifier) pairs?
  Q: or can we assume that if we are in a PolyType, it is FORALL?
  A: ASSUME FORALL, and store no quantifier at all. In anthill every binder is attached
     to a DECLARATION, never to a free-floating type — `operation map[Dst, EffP](…)`,
     `sort [F] { … }`, `sort T = ?`. There is no position in which a mixed binder list
     could arise, and §5.6 already says an operation type parameter is "the CALLER's to
     instantiate", which is forall by construction. Storing a per-binder quantifier
     would additionally make an ILLEGAL STATE REPRESENTABLE: the polarity rule makes the
     quantifier a function of POSITION (parameter = forall, return = exists), so a
     binder tagged exists could sit in a forall position and the two would disagree.
     Store what is NOT derivable — the name and the BOUND.

  THE EXISTENTIAL NEEDS NO BINDER NODE. At a declaration it is implied by the return
  position; at a use it has ALREADY BEEN OPENED into a rigid, which is exactly the
  Skolem variant this ticket is about. The one candidate exception is `ensures Spec[C]`
  (WI-402), the EXPLICIT existential the loader rewrites away into a bare return — an
  `Exists` node is what would let that spelling survive instead of buying nothing
  (WI-1063's words).

  A BINDER CARRIES NO BOUND, corrected 2026-08-11 after an earlier draft of this note
  proposed `TypeVarDecl(name, bound)`. CHECKED IN THE GRAMMAR: `operation_type_param` is
  `seq(field('name', identifier), optional(seq('=', field('default', _type))))`, and the
  `= default` arm is parsed ONLY to give a good diagnostic — WI-850 refuses it, and
  kernel-language §5.4 says `TypeParam ::= Name`. `sort_type_param`'s optional list is
  the HIGHER-KINDED member list (`F[T, …]`), not a bound either. So NEITHER binder form
  can carry one, and a `bound` field would be permanently empty.

  BOUNDS EXIST ONLY AT SORT LEVEL AND ARE ALREADY REFLECTED: a sort's `requires Eq[T]`
  (and the `effects E` desugaring, `sort E = ? requires EffectsRuntime[Effects = E]`) is
  a SEPARATE clause, emitted as `SortRequiresInfo`. It is not part of the binder and must
  not be duplicated into one.

  POLYTYPE IS A FORM OF TYPE, NOT A FIELD ON AN OPERATION (user, 2026-08-11, correcting
  an earlier draft of this ticket that proposed `OperationInfo(…, type_params:
  List[Symbol])`). It is a `TypeExtractor` VARIANT, sitting beside `Arrow`,
  `Parameterized` and `NamedTuple`:

    entity PolyType(binders: List[Symbol], body: Term)      -- forall by construction
    entity Skolem(id: Symbol)                               -- an OPENED existential
    entity FlexVar(id: Symbol)                              -- an INSTANTIATED forall
    (optional) entity Exists(binders: List[Symbol], body: Term)
                                                            -- only to stop `ensures
                                                               Spec[C]` being erased

    empty : PolyType([A], Arrow(NamedTuple([]), List[A], {}, 0))

  THE DIFFERENCE IS NOT COSMETIC, and the eta-lift is the proof. A field on
  `OperationInfo` can only ever describe an OPERATION; a type FORM composes — it appears
  anywhere a type can. `typing.rs:6271` excludes a type-parameterized operation from
  having a function-value form because "its arrow would carry unfreshened type-param vars
  that alias across multiple eta-lifts of the same op". A `type_params` FIELD could not
  fix that at all: a function VALUE has no `OperationInfo`, it has a TYPE. Only a
  polymorphic type form can be that value's type, with freshening at instantiation.

  SO `OperationInfo` PROBABLY NEEDS NO NEW FIELD. Its type IS the PolyType, and
  `params` / `return_type` / `effects` are a decomposition of that type's body. Whether
  the info record should keep the decomposition, carry the assembled type, or both, is a
  question for the implementation — but "add type_params to OperationInfo" is NOT this
  ticket's deliverable and an earlier draft was wrong to state it as one.

  AN OPERATION IS A FUNCTION FROM A NAMED TUPLE (user, 2026-08-11), and the reflect
  layer already says so: `TypeExtractor.Arrow(param, result, effects, arity)`, where
  WI-791's rule is "at arity one `param` is the sole parameter's TYPE, at any other
  arity it is the parameter LIST — a `NamedTuple` of exactly `arity` components". So an
  operation's TYPE is already an arrow over a named tuple, and `OperationInfo`'s
  separate `params` / `return_type` / `effects` fields are a DECOMPOSITION of one type
  rather than three independent facts.

  THAT REFRAMING HAS A MEASURED PAYOFF, and it changes which target is worth building.
  An arrow has nowhere to put binders, and the consequence is a user-visible capability
  gap rather than a representation nicety — `typing.rs:6271`, in the eta-lift gate:

      // Only a type-parameterized op stays excluded: its arrow would carry unfreshened
      // type-param vars that alias across multiple eta-lifts of the same op.
      if !op.type_params.is_empty() { return None; }

  So EVERY type-parameterized stdlib operation — `Iterable.map`, `find`, `filter`,
  `FiniteCollection.foldLeft`, the finite-combinator `collect`/`iterator` — CANNOT BE
  PASSED AS A FUNCTION VALUE. WI-1063 recorded the same fact from the other end ("an
  arrow type has nowhere to write exists; it is why an operation with type parameters
  has no function-value form either") and its eta site opens once per LIFT rather than
  once per application for the same reason.

  WHICH MEANS THE MINIMAL SHAPE ABOVE IS NOT ENOUGH TO BUY ANYTHING. A
  `type_params: List[Symbol]` field RECORDS the binders but puts them nowhere the arrow
  can use, so the aliasing that forces the exclusion is untouched and the restriction
  stays. `PolyType(binders, Arrow(NamedTuple(params), result, effects, arity))` is the
  shape where per-use instantiation freshens the binders, the aliasing disappears, and
  the eta gate's second clause can be RETIRED — which is a capability delivered, not a
  field added. Scope it as: the minimal field closes this ticket's measured defect
  (reflect cannot see the binders); the PolyType wrap is the increment that lifts the
  function-value restriction, and it should carry that restriction's removal as its
  acceptance rather than being justified by tidiness.

  WHERE THE BINDER SITS DECIDES `empty`'s RESULT TYPE (user's question, 2026-08-11).
  Under a PolyType the quantifier is on the ARROW, not on the return:

      empty : forall A. () -> List[A]
            = PolyType([A], Arrow(NamedTuple([]), List[A], {}, 0))

  so the result type is `List[A]` with `A` bound by the enclosing forall, and each USE
  instantiates it freshly. That is the fresh-per-use behaviour already measured, but
  DECLARED instead of emergent — and it collapses the three different mechanisms the
  corpus currently relies on into one:

    mk() -> List            at namespace level   REFUSED  (no binder: the return's
                                                           unwritten slot is exists,
                                                           opened to a rigid — correct)
    List.empty() -> List    inside `sort List`   works    (self-sort tie: the slot is
                                                           List's own T)
    LogicalStream.empty()
      -> LogicalStream[?A]                       works    (named-variable exemption —
                                                           WI-1078's hole)

  THE `Nothing` OPTION ANSWERS THE SAME QUESTION DIFFERENTLY, and for a NULLARY producer
  it may be the better answer. Both measured:

    forall A     `mk3[A]() -> List[T = A]` loads and serves Int64 and String in one
                 program. COST: where nothing pins A the call is a loud "expected a type
                 for 'A', got unconstrained — use `mk[A = …](…)`", so `empty()` with no
                 expectation in scope is an error.
    List[Nothing]  `mk6() -> List[T = Nothing]` loads. The result type is CONCRETE, so it
                 never needs pinning; usability comes from `Covariant(sort: List, param:
                 T)` by subtyping.

  For an EMPTY collection the second is arguably more honest — there is a canonical
  element type, the uninhabited one, so nothing needs inferring; the forall version says
  "any A" and then demands the context supply one for a value with no elements to
  disagree about. The tradeoff is which machinery pays: forall needs instantiation,
  `Nothing` needs variance. NOT MEASURED, and the thing to check before choosing: whether
  the declared `Covariant` fact is actually ENFORCED at this position, or only recorded.

  WHAT THIS DOES NOT GIVE WI-1077, stated because the earlier draft implied it did: a
  bounded binder is NOT delivered here, so "let a declaration say which parameter is the
  carrier" still needs NEW SURFACE (`sort C = ? carrier`, or a `spec` keyword — WI-1077's
  own options (a)/(b)). The WI-1077 -> WI-1079 dependency still holds, but for the weaker
  reason: this ticket builds the binder LAYER that such a marker would attach to and be
  reflected through, not the marker.

  SYNTAX, asked and answered: `[A]List[A]` COLLIDES. grammar.js's collection_literal
  says it in as many words — "Bare [...] = collection literal, Name[...] = application
  (disambiguated by leading Name)" — and since types are terms the two share one
  grammar, so a leading bare `[A]` parses as a collection containing `A`. Every binder
  the language has is introduced by a KEYWORD, never bare. A free-standing quantified
  type would therefore need a keyword prefix — but it may not be wanted at all, since
  fixing `OperationInfo.type_params` IS most of PolyType and needs NO new syntax: the
  binders are already written, merely not reflected.

THE SCOPE QUESTION IS SETTLED, AND NOT BY A REACHABILITY MEASUREMENT (user, 2026-08-11:
"we should represent all in reflect layer. Its why it exists"). An earlier draft of this
ticket made "does a skolem ever REACH `extract` today?" the first task, with a loud arm
and a seven-tier run as the instrument. That is the wrong gate: the reflect layer's job
IS to represent the language's types, so a form it cannot express is a defect whether or
not today's corpus reaches it. Reachability may still be worth knowing for PRIORITY; it
is not the acceptance.

AND THE GAP IS DEMONSTRABLY NOT HYPOTHETICAL — WI-1076 IS ITS BUG. A declared type
PARAMETER appears in reflect as `SortRef(T)`, indistinguishable from a concrete sort:
that is exactly why `List provides Stream[T]` filed its carrier at `T`, because
`provision_carrier_binding` filters the binding's base on `SymbolKind::Sort` and a type
parameter PASSES that filter. So the conflation already shipped a defect, one ticket ago.

THREE FORMS NEED REPRESENTING, and only the first is a new construct:

  a BOUND variable        `forall A. () -> List[A]`   no binder exists anywhere today,
                                                     and `A` in the return reads as
                                                     `SortRef(A)`            -> PolyType
                                                                                + bound-var
  an INSTANTIATED FLEX    `?A1`, the type of          `TypeExtractor::Error`  -> flex-var
                          `empty()` at a use          (MEASURED)                 form
  an OPENED RIGID         `rho`, the type of          `TypeExtractor::Error`  -> skolem
                          `widen(s)` at a use         (MEASURED)                 form

PolyType covers the first and gives the other two a provenance: INSTANTIATING a PolyType
yields the flex, OPENING an existential yields the rigid. That is why it is the chosen
direction rather than three unrelated variants — the three forms are one lifecycle, and
representing them as one keeps a reader from having to reconstruct which is which.

OPTIONS FOR THE FIRST STEP, smallest first:
  1. ADD A FIELD — `TypeVar(name: Symbol, rigid: Bool)`. Smallest, but touches every
     construction site of `make_type_var` and every reader that matches `TypeVar`.
  2. ADD A VARIANT — `entity Skolem(id: Symbol)` beside `TypeVar`, and a `type_head`
     arm for the bare-variable carrier. Keeps `TypeVar`'s readers untouched and makes
     the two genuinely different things different, which is this repo's stated
     preference (make illegal state unrepresentable over check logic).
  3. DECIDE SKOLEMS NEVER REACH REFLECT and say so at `extract_type`, with (a)'s
     measurement as the evidence and a loud arm to keep it true.

Option 2 also gives WI-1078 somewhere to put its answer: if a named `?E` in a return
opens, the thing it opens TO needs a name in the reflect layer.

ACCEPTANCE: (a) is measured and reported — whether a skolem reaches `extract` in any
corpus tier, counted at the site rather than argued; the chosen option is implemented
and `extract_type` on a `Var::Rigid` no longer answers `Error` (or the decision that it
may is written at the function with the measurement behind it); a test DRIVES the
distinction — a skolem and a flexible variable must extract to different things, which
is the assertion that fails today for both directions at once; kernel-language's
§"In a RETURN the quantifier flips to exists" says how an opened slot is represented,
since it currently describes the semantics and not the datum; each test says which case
fails when the change is backed out. cargo-test green via rustland/scripts/test.sh.

REFERENCE: WI-1063 (the opening, and `UnwrittenFill::Anonymous` which mints the skolem),
WI-1078 (the same flex/rigid conflation at the rule level), WI-1061/WI-1059 (the
projection filler, the one skolem that DOES have a reflect form — `ExprCarried`, because
the language spells `s.T`), WI-428 (`RigidTypeProjection`, a projection off a rigid
subject, which is NOT a rigid variable), WI-540 (the `TypeExtractor` sort), WI-391 (the
"every backing term classifies into exactly one structural variant" invariant this
ticket says is unmet for a variable carrier).
