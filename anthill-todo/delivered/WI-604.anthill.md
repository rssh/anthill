## Description

Typer: a qualified Iterable consumer (isEmpty / find) over a bare lazy Iterable.map(...) result leaks the produced stream's access row as 's.E' instead of grounding it to '{}'. Repro: 'operation f(xs: List[T = Int64]) -> Bool = Iterable.isEmpty(Iterable.map(xs, inc))' fails to load with 'type mismatch in f.effects: expected declared: [], got undeclared effect: s.E' (find leaks the same way). A 'let m : Stream[T = Int64, E = {}] = Iterable.map(xs, inc)' annotation does NOT fix it (the leak just becomes 'm.E') — so the bare-Stream-typed value's written/inferred E={} is not substituted into the consumer's effect row; the consumer re-projects receiver.E. ASYMMETRY (unexplained, the crux): the now-removed Iterable.size grounded the same expression to '{}' even though its body 'Stream.count(iterator(c))' is structurally identical to isEmpty's 'Stream.isEmpty(iterator(c))'. Surfaced by WI-589 (finiteness Phase C) when repurposing wi492 away from the removed Iterable.size; worked around there by counting via transitive Iterable.iterator(...) + bounded takeN (sound but indirect). ACCEPTANCE: 'Iterable.isEmpty(Iterable.map([1,2,3,4], inc))' on a pure List typechecks PURE (access row grounds to {}), with a regression test pinning isEmpty AND find over a bare lazy MappedStream; then wi492's lazy_map_iterator_count can revert to the direct Iterable.isEmpty form.
