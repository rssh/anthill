## description

REVIEW INSTANCE SEARCH OVER CHAINS OF PARAMETRIZED `requires` AND `provides` — is a multi-hop answer found, and found with the right bindings?

RAISED BY THE USER during WI-1110 (2026-08-16), and it is a REVIEW ticket, not a defect report: the shapes below are NOT measured. WI-1110 established that a spec's `provides` is a conversion and took it out of candidate collection; the user's point is that the relation is more general than the one hop that change reasoned about — a goal may be answerable only through a CHAIN of `requires` and `provides` edges, parametrized at every hop, with the actual provider at the far end. Whether today's search finds those is unmeasured, and WI-1110 did not widen or narrow it deliberately.

WHAT THE SEARCH DOES TODAY, read off the code rather than remembered.
  * `collect_provides_candidates` (kb/typing.rs) matches a goal against `SortProvidesInfo` rows whose spec BASE equals the goal's, then filters by binding. It is ONE HOP: the row's `sort_ref` becomes the candidate `impl_sort`.
  * TWO HOPS exist only as a narrow fallback (WI-714 / proposal 052) and only when the goal HAS a carrier: `!carrier_directly_provides && sort_provides(carrier, impl_sort)`. Its documented population is exactly `Relation provides LogicalStream provides Stream`.
  * Everything else multi-hop is EAGER, not searched: `derive_forwarded_provisions` materializes a direct row per carrier per forwarded floor, in a bounded fixpoint (`ROUNDS = 8`), for rows passing `is_identity_forwarding` — every binding a same-named type parameter (`[T = T, E = E]`).
  * The caller's own chain is a SEPARATE route (`ResolutionScope.available_requires`, the Strategy 1/2/3 projection search), which is where a `requires` in scope answers a goal.

SO THE QUESTIONS TO ANSWER, each with a fixture that DRIVES a call and asserts a value — not a clean load (WI-948):
 1. A NON-IDENTITY forwarding chain. `sort F { sort A = ?  sort B = ?  provides Sp[X = B, Y = A] }` permutes; a renamed one (`provides Sp[X = A]`) is the simpler case. Neither is an identity forwarding, so NOTHING is derived for a carrier of `F` — and after WI-1110 neither is a conversion, so `F` stays a candidate and becomes the `impl_sort`, which supplies no operation. Is the goal answered, refused, or answered by a sort that cannot run it?
 2. DEPTH. Three hops with identity bindings settle in the fixpoint; three hops where the MIDDLE one renames do not. Where does the answer stop being found, and is the failure loud?
 3. BINDINGS ACROSS HOPS. When a chain does answer, are the bindings composed correctly end to end, or only at the last hop? `Pair provides Ord[Pair] :- Ord[A], Ord[B]` composes conditions; a chain of forwardings composes SUBSTITUTIONS, and the two are different machinery.
 4. `requires`-THEN-`provides`. The user's own phrasing: a chain of `requires` whose last link provides. Does the caller-chain route (`available_requires` + projection) compose across more than one hop of parametrized requires, and does it meet the provider route at the end?
 5. IS EAGER DERIVATION THE RIGHT SHAPE AT ALL? It exists because the search is one hop. If the search followed a spec-to-spec edge properly — resolving `WeakOrd[X]` by recursing into `Ord[X]`, which is what the edge MEANS — the derivation would be redundant, and WI-1110's candidate exclusion might be too. That is the alternative to weigh, not a foregone conclusion: eager rows are read by `build_sort_ops_table` and the runtime index, which a lazy edge would not populate.

WHY IT IS WORTH ASKING NOW. WI-1110 made the ONE-HOP reading load-bearing in a new way: a conversion is excluded from the search on the argument that the derivation relocated its answer, and that argument is only as wide as `is_identity_forwarding`. Anything outside it is neither derived nor searched, and nothing in the corpus writes such a shape — so a green suite says nothing (WI-979). The invariant is stated at `provision_is_conversion` and `self_supplied_entries`; this ticket is whether the invariant is enough.

ACCEPTANCE: each numbered question answered by a driven fixture with an asserted value or an asserted refusal, and the refusals must be TRUTHFUL (a shape the search cannot reach must say so, not report a cycle or an unrelated missing requirement — the diagnostic wart WI-1110 removed for the identity case). If a genuine wrong answer is found, fix it; if the search is correct as-is, record WHY at `collect_provides_candidates` so the next reader does not re-derive it. REFERENCE: WI-1110; proposal 052 (WI-714); 058 §3.2, §3.8.

FOLDED IN 2026-08-16 (user): SELF-SUPPLY IS A PROJECTION, NOT A SEARCH — and it is question 5 seen from the other end, so the two must be decided together.

WHAT SHIPS TODAY. WI-1110 gave a spec's `provides` a chain entry marked `SupplySource::SelfSupplied`, and then filled that slot exactly like a `requires` one: `provider_requires_subgoals` turns it into a `SortGoal` and the resolver SEARCHES for a provider, which the carrier's eagerly-derived row answers. At construction the two supply sources are deliberately indistinguishable, and the field is read at one place only — telling a conversion from a membership claim.

WHAT THE MODEL ACTUALLY SAYS. 'The dictionary is BUILT FROM SELF' means the slot is a PROJECTION of the dictionary we already hold, not a goal. An `Ord[Int64]` dictionary IS a `WeakOrd[Int64]` one plus a law; nothing needs resolving to get from one to the other.

WHY IT IS NOT COSMETIC — IT IS WHY THE EXCLUSION EXISTS. Because the slot starts a search, the same edge is traversed both ways: `Eq provides PartialEq` says PartialEq comes FROM Eq, and Eq's chain says Eq CONTAINS PartialEq. That is the measured `construction is cyclic: PartialEq[X] -> PartialEq[X]` (1876 failures with the skip removed). Excluding conversions from candidate collection is what breaks the loop. If the slot were a projection no search would start there, the loop would not exist, and `collect_provides_candidates` might need no special case at all — which is exactly what question 5 asks from the search side.

AND IT COMPOSES WITH 5 RATHER THAN COMPETING. If the search FOLLOWS a spec-to-spec edge (resolve `WeakOrd[X]` by recursing into `Ord[X]`, which is what the edge means) AND the resulting slot is a projection, then: at a real carrier `Ord[Int64]` resolves to `Int64` and the WeakOrd dictionary is a projection of it — no bogus `impl_sort`, no cycle; at an abstract element `Ord[?T]` has no provider and fails TRUTHFULLY. Both of WI-1110's symptoms dissolve without either the eager derivation or the candidate skip. That is the shape to weigh, not to assume: it has real costs, below.

COSTS TO MEASURE, not to argue.
 * DICTIONARY ARITY MOVES AGAIN. A projected slot may need no slot at all. wi857's `the_layout_counts_what_resolve_bundles` has already gone 2 -> 3 (WI-1109) -> 1 (WI-1110); this would move it a third time, and the count IS the assertion there.
 * THE EAGER ROWS HAVE OTHER READERS. `derive_forwarded_provisions` does not only feed the search: `build_sort_ops_table` inherits the forwarded spec's operations onto every carrier that gained a row (measured at its site — it is what makes `WeakOrd.compare` dispatch on `Int64`), and the persisted runtime index reads the same relation. A lazy edge populates neither. Dropping the derivation is therefore NOT free even if the search stops needing it.
 * PROJECTION AT EVAL. A slot filled by projection has to be projected somewhere — frame push, `DictLayout::slots_for`, `synth_req_names`. WI-1110 chose search precisely because it needed no change there; that choice is what is being reopened.
 * `SupplySource` BECOMES LOAD-BEARING RATHER THAN A LABEL. Today it is read once. Under projection it decides how a slot is FILLED, which is what the user's model said in the first place.
