Refuse a supplier tie at LOAD on the BODY-LESS spec-op path too, or record why its declining arms may not. WI-1012 gave the refusal a load face for the DEFAULTED half of a two-half mechanism: its arm sits inside 'check_apply_iter's 'lookup_spec_op_dispatch(kb, fn_sym).is_none()' block, so it covers exactly the ops eval's 'resolve_carrier_override_by_value' serves. The BODY-LESS half - eval's 'resolve_spec_op_target_by_value', the reader WI-842 built - has no load face, and three arms of the same 'check_apply_iter' hand a STATICALLY CONCRETE carrier to it without refusing: (a) the 'DispatchOutcome::Unique' arm's 'if impl_op_sym != fn_sym && op_has_runnable_body(..)' has NO else, so a host-mapped or builtin-backed impl (WI-876) leaves the call as the spec op; (b) the concrete 'NoCandidates' arm deliberately leaves the call untagged; (c) 'Deferred' with no matching slot. 'DispatchOutcome::Ambiguous' does NOT cover these: it ties PROVISIONS matching the goal, whereas route 1 (the carrier's OWN member) is registered by 'build_sort_ops_table' pass 1 regardless of any provision, so route 1 is invisible to it and 'spec_op_suppliers_for_carrier' can hold two where the provision walk holds one. NOT DRIVEN - this is a structural reading from a WI-1012 review agent, and reaching >=2 there needs route 1 to disagree with the provision count. FIRST WORK IS THEREFORE A FIXTURE, not a fix: build a program that makes one of (a)/(b)/(c) reach two suppliers and confirm it currently answers 'AmbiguousSpecOpDispatch' at the call; if none can, the answer is a comment at each arm saying so, and this ticket closes as recorded rather than fixed. IF IT REPRODUCES: the shared refusal already exists - 'render_suppliers' + 'supplier_tie_repair' + 'TypeError::AmbiguousSpecOpDispatch' - so the fix is one helper called from the WI-444 arm and the reproducing arm(s), all of which already hold the span, carrier and op. Note the repair differs by half and is already typed for it: a body-less op HAS a dispatch slot, so a witness rival is 'SupplierTieRepair::NameableWitness' there, which the defaulted half can never be. ALSO IN SCOPE, from the same review: WI-1012's refusal is keyed per CALL SITE (the span is in 'dedup_key'), so N call sites give N errors; 'AmbiguousEqDispatch' does the declaration-level thing once at the index build. A declaration-level check is NOT simply better here - WI-1010 rejected refusing the declaration pair because 'check_instance_fact_op_signatures' has just certified the binding and WI-859 admitted the same pair as one dictionary written twice - but the reasoning should be written down once rather than re-derived. ACCEPTANCE: either a driven fixture plus the refusal extended to it, or a comment at each of the three arms recording that no tie reaches them and why.
