## Description

Operation body as the single source of truth -- derive rule views, retire hand-written duplicates.

PRINCIPLE (design fork agreed 2026-06-28 with user): an operation's BODY is the one definition. Its equational (<=>), proof, and relational (Prolog-style) views should be DERIVED from the body, not hand-written alongside it. A hand-written rule that duplicates a body is redundant and can drift -- or diverge from it unsoundly.

EVIDENCE -- stdlib/anthill/prelude/list.anthill:
- append: the two <=> rules are the operation body's match arms restated verbatim (nil -> ys ; cons(x,rest) -> cons(x, append(rest,ys))) -- pure duplication, mechanically derivable from the body.
- member: NOT a safe duplicate. The body branches on eq(head,x) (the Eq op); the rules branch on cons(head: ?x, ...) (UNIFICATION). For a type with a custom Eq (eq != structural equality) the unification rules give WRONG answers -- a latent soundness gap. Deriving the relational form from the eq-body makes membership use the declared Eq consistently (and, owed-on-consumption, the relational query then correctly requires Eq[T]).

SCOPE:
1. Derive an operation's <=>/rewrite + relational forms from its body ("rule is THE knowledge primitive": an op body desugars to an eval strategy PLUS its rules).
2. Retire hand-written rules that duplicate a body (append) or unsoundly diverge from it (member).
3. Extra LAWS (e.g. append associativity) are NOT hand-written defining rules -- they are THEOREMS proved from the body.
4. Hand-written rules survive only for genuine STANDALONE relations (predicates with no operation body).

RELATION to WI-502 / WI-578: separable. The typed-value carrier (WI-578) is unaffected (it is about how a value carries its type, however the rule arose). But this tightens the typed-rule design: derived rules are typed by the body (signature + owed-on-consumption), so the explicit [E]/?x:T typed-pattern surface is needed only for bodyless relations. See docs/design/constrained-term-substrate.md "Typed rule patterns -- surface and matching".

ACCEPTANCE: design-first -- a proposal/design note specifying (a) the body -> rule derivation, and (b) the retirement of the list.anthill append/member duplicates, including member's eq-vs-unification soundness fix. Implementation of the derivation is a follow-on.
