## Description

DECIDE WHAT `anthill.prelude.Bool.ite` IS — it runs on NEITHER route, and WI-884 measured that the recorded justification covers only one of them. THE MEASUREMENT (`wi884_sibling_backing_test.rs`): `Bool.ite(true, 1, 2)` in an operation body dies `OperationBodyMissing`, and the same call as a rule-body goal (`eq(Bool.ite(true, 10, 20), 10)`) yields ZERO solutions in BOTH orientations, beside a control goal that yields one. So `ite` reduces NOWHERE on a program that loads clean — WI-881's defect shape, on the one operation WI-884's audit could not close. THE VALUE-LEVEL HALF IS DELIBERATE and stands: an operation's arguments are evaluated BEFORE the call, so a registered `ite` evaluates BOTH branches, which is not what a conditional means; `if`/`then`/`else` is the lazy value-level form. `eval/builtins.rs`'s header says so, and `ordered.anthill` records the same decision from the other side (it writes `max`'s LAW with `ite` and `max`'s BODY with `if`, and says why). THE RULE-LEVEL HALF WAS FALSE: that same header claimed 'rule-level uses of `ite` are handled by the prelude's rewrite rules during SLD resolution'. The cause is that `bool.anthill`'s two case laws (`ite_true`, `ite_false`) are UNTAGGED, and an untagged equation is INERT — WI-881's headline recurring one file over. NOT the `=` spelling, which was WI-884's first diagnosis and was REFUTED by driving (that divergence is WI-888). WHY TAGGING THEM IS NOT THE FIX, MEASURED: a `[simp]` head matches STRUCTURALLY, so `ite(true, ?t, ?_)` has a redex only where the CONDITION IS ALREADY A LITERAL. Driven on a control (`a_simp_ite_reaches_only_a_literal_condition`), `myIte(true, 10, 20)` inlines to 10 while `myIte(Int64.gte(a, b), 10, 20)` — the shape every real use has, and the shape `ordered.anthill`'s own `max` law writes — still dies. Half-backed is WORSE than dead: it looks backed. THE OPTIONS, each with its cost. (a) Tag the two laws anyway and document the literal-only limit — cheapest, and it leaves `ordered.anthill`'s `max` law no more executable than today. (b) Give `ite` a value-level body (`= if cond then then else else`) and accept STRICTNESS: it then runs everywhere, and `ite(neq(b, 0), div(a, b), 0)` RAISES where today it dies loudly — a changed meaning that wants a name saying it is strict. (c) DELETE `ite` from `Bool` in favour of a term-level conditional that is not an operation; the users to carry are `ordered.anthill` (`max`/`min`), `int64.anthill` (`sign`), and `docs/design/abstract-interpreter-and-rules.md`:272, which plans to translate the `Bool.ite` FUNCTOR to SMT `ite` — that SMT use is the reason `ite` probably has to survive in some form. ALSO SETTLE THE DIAGNOSTIC: today an unbacked-BY-DESIGN operation and an unwritten one both die `OperationBodyMissing`, which reads as 'not implemented yet' and sent WI-884 looking for a missing implementation that was never meant to exist. ACCEPTANCE: every operation `bool.anthill` declares either runs or is deleted (WI-884's own acceptance, for the one it left open); if `ite` survives with no value-level implementation, the failure NAMES why instead of reporting a missing body; a test drives whichever route the decision keeps; full workspace green via rustland/scripts/test.sh. REFERENCE: WI-884 (the three measurements and their controls), WI-881 ([simp] is the enablement), WI-888 (the head-connective divergence that made the wrong diagnosis plausible), `ordered.anthill`'s note above its `max`/`min` laws.
