## Description

A WRITTEN one-component tuple type never 1-collapses, but every COMPUTED one-column result always does -- so the natural spelling of a one-column result is unsatisfiable. SITE: collapse_schema (rustland/anthill-core/src/kb/typing.rs, the arm '1 => columns[0].1.clone()', which drops the column NAME). Since WI-766 made '(a: A)' writable the two sides can now disagree, and every disagreement is a rejection. THREE MEASURED, all loud: (1) 'operation as_tuple() -> (a: Int64) = resid()' where resid() -> Without[T = (a: Int64, b: String), Drop = (b: String)] gives 'expected (a: Int64), got Int64'; (2) the same for a one-entry Project keep spec: 'expected (who: String), got String'; (3) 'operation who() -> Relation[T = (name: String)] = Person.adult' over a one-column rule gives 'expected Relation[T = (name: String)], got Relation[T = String, E = ...]'. Consequence beyond ergonomics: Concat and Without stop being inverses at arity 1 -- Concat[(a: Int64), (b: String)] builds the 2-tuple, but dropping b back off yields the bare element, not (a: Int64). NOT a soundness hole (every rejection is loud and located) but the surface now admits spellings that are guaranteed wrong at every use, with no diagnostic explaining WHY. DECIDE which way to make them agree: either a one-column computed schema keeps its column and stops collapsing (check what depends on the collapse first -- wi763_single_component_keep_spec_reduces_and_collapses asserts it, and relation drain/materialize may too), or the collapse stays and the typer explains it at the mismatch ('a one-column schema collapses to its element type; write A, not (a: A)'). docs/kernel-language.md now records the disagreement in the 1-collapse section; whichever way this lands, that text and the wi763 test must follow. ACCEPTANCE: the two sides agree, or the mismatch names the collapse; full workspace green via scripts/test.sh.
